Correlating Resonance: see the frames in your coded corpus

CorRes shows the frames in a coded corpus. It finds which of your concepts appear together more often than their frequencies predict, tests whether those groupings hold up when your documents are split in half, and shows who voices each one. It doesn't code text: it works on a file you've already coded, with one row per document and one column per concept.

Start with your own coded file, or try one of our sample data sets to see how it works.

How to read this picture

Each row and each column is one of your concepts, in the same order, so the picture is mirrored along the diagonal. The square for A and B is the same as the square for B and A. The diagonal is blank, because a concept isn't compared with itself.

Each square shows residual co-occurrence: how much more, or less, often two concepts appear in the same document than their frequencies alone would predict. Red means together more than expected, blue less than expected, and white about as expected. The deeper the colour, the bigger the difference.
