How Correlating Resonance reads a corpus

corres works on departures from independence, not on counts. Two concepts that both appear everywhere will co-occur constantly, and that tells you nothing. corres looks for the pairs that appear together more often, or less often, than chance predicts. That is the difference between finding structure and rediscovering which of your codes are common.

It will tell you when the answer is no

Before corres interprets anything, it tests whether your corpus can support interpretation at all. It resamples your documents, reruns the analysis on each half, and checks whether the groups survive. If they do not, it stops and tells you why — with the diagnosis, and every concept and document it set aside, itemised.

Two of the corpora we most wanted to use have been declined by our own method, for two different reasons.

A method that cannot say no, cannot warn you. Knowing that before you build on it is the point.

What you get

Groups that survived the stability test. A role for every concept — which ones sit at the core of a group, which ones bridge, which ones sit apart. And the parameters and conditions each result was produced under, so the run can be reproduced.

What is yours

Our own papers read groups like these as frames. That is our interpretation, and it has not been tested against an external referent — so corres returns the structure and leaves the reading to you. That part of the work is yours.

What it costs

corres is free while it is in beta, and there is nothing to set up — no account, no key, no billing. Your matrix is sent to our server to be analysed by a fixed set of python scripts and is not retained afterwards. The analysis runs from your matrix by fixed rules, so two people running the same matrix under the same conditions get the same answer.

Where this sits

corres is the first application built on the Correlating Resonance methodology. The stages upstream of it — building a typology from a corpus, and coding a full corpus against that typology — are ones we use internally to prepare our own corpora, and are in development.

corres is in beta and under active development. Some thresholds are declared provisional and are under review; every run reports the conditions it ran under.

Tell us what you think

corres is open and free during beta, and we would rather you ran your own material through it than took our word for any of this. We are most interested in the runs that fail. If corres declines your corpus, that is a result, and it is the one we most want to hear about — what you brought, what came back, and whether the diagnosis told you anything you could use.
