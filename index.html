A method for finding structure in coded qualitative data.

Correlating Resonance takes a corpus you have already coded and reports how the concepts in it hang together — which travel as a group, which bridge between groups, and where your material divides.

It works on departures from independence, not on counts. Two concepts that both appear everywhere will co-occur constantly, and that tells you nothing. corres looks for the pairs that appear together more often, or less often, than chance predicts.

What it asks of you

A document–term matrix: one row per document, one column per concept, coded by you or by your own process. corres does not code your corpus for you, and it does not interpret your results. It takes coded data and returns structure, along with the parameters and conditions that structure was produced under.

Where it stands

Before corres interprets anything, it tests whether your corpus can support interpretation at all. If it cannot, corres stops and says why — with the diagnosis, and every concept and document it set aside, itemised.

Two of the corpora we most wanted to use have been declined by our own method, for two different reasons, and we report both refusals. It also found a defect in work we had already published. The method is corrected; that earlier analysis is being redone.

Our own papers read groups like these as frames. That is our interpretation, and it has not been tested against an external referent — so corres returns the structure and leaves the reading to you.

corres is in beta and under active development. Some thresholds are declared provisional and are under review; every run reports the conditions it ran under.
